Scams depend on speed. FirstRing adds a pause.

A scam call can put savings at risk, and with them, the independence of running your own affairs. Scammers don’t succeed because people aren’t careful. They succeed by rushing: a code “right now,” a payment “before it’s too late.”

FirstRing takes that pressure off. It answers first, lets the caller explain, and leaves you a clear summary, so you decide what happens next with time to think.

For families and caregivers, it means knowing what happened on a call without listening in on every conversation.

  • Never reads out a sign-in or verification code.

  • Never agrees to gift cards, wires or unusual payments.

  • Never lets anyone into your computer or phone.

How it works

  1. 1

    FirstRing answers.

    A calm, clearly synthetic voice picks up unfamiliar calls, so you don’t have to.

  2. 2

    It checks the request.

    It listens for what the caller actually wants and stops requests for codes, unusual payments or device access.

  3. 3

    You stay in control.

    You read a short, plain summary of each call and decide whether to call back, on a number you already trust.

Your protection is the first ring. What it learns can help the next one.

One warning can help the next family.

When FirstRing stops a suspicious request, it can share a short, expiring description of the trick with other participating households: what was asked for, what the caller claimed to be, and how they pushed. Never your name, your number, or a recording.

Other households’ FirstRing checks new calls against it. Scammers change phone numbers easily. Changing the trick is harder.

A warning spreads like a ripple, reaching households that haven’t seen the trick yet.

Not every unwanted call is a scam.

Unwanted call
A real caller you’d rather not talk to right now. FirstRing takes a message; you decide.
Spam
Repeated sales or robocalls. Annoying, but usually not after your accounts.
Suspected fraud
Asks for codes, unusual payments or device access. FirstRing declines, saves a note, and can share the tactic.

How a warning could grow stronger

Concept · not live yet

An illustration of a planned design for reported numbers. Today’s prototype shares tactic warnings only; it does not score or block phone numbers.

  1. 1

    One report

    A blocked call creates a minimized report. On its own, it changes nothing for anyone else.

  2. 2

    Independent reports agree

    Separate households report the same tactic. Each household counts once.

  3. 3

    A warning appears

    Calls showing that number are screened more carefully and labeled “reported number.”

  4. 4

    Stronger screening

    Inside participating FirstRing services only. FirstRing can’t shut down a number or a carrier account.

How this would stay fair
No duplicate counting
Repeat reports from one household, or about one call, count once.
Corrections
Anyone flagged by mistake can ask for a review, and a correction removes the warning everywhere.
Expiry
Warnings fade unless new, independent reports renew them. Today’s tactic warnings expire after 24 hours.
Spoofed and reused numbers
A number can be faked or reassigned, so a warning never claims who called. The tactic check keeps working when the number changes.
Our goal: help put phone scammers out of business by making their tricks harder to repeat.

Could someone copy my voice?

Any audio played to another person can be recorded. That includes a voice assistant’s voice, and yours. So FirstRing doesn’t put your voice on the line.

  • A distinct synthetic voice, by default

    FirstRing answers in its own preset assistant voice. Custom voice cloning is turned off in this prototype.

  • Familiar isn’t proof

    Voices can be imitated. Sounding like a grandchild, a bank or a doctor never permits a code, a payment or access. When in doubt, hang up and call back on a number you already know.

  • Only what’s needed is shared

    Call summaries stay private to your dashboard. Community warnings carry the tactic only, never names, numbers, recordings or transcripts.
